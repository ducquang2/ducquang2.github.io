I leave home at 7 in the morning, start work at 8 and get back around 7 in the evening. That doesn't leave much time, and there's a lot I want to fit into it: two gym sessions a week, about six hours of schoolwork, four side projects in progress, some rest, plus all the small chores that squeeze in between.

I used to handle them the obvious way: write them on paper or in the notes app on my phone. Writing things down is quick, but afterwards I still had to sit and work out what goes on which day and at what time. For a bit over a month now I've handed that sorting to AI. This post is a ten-day log of using it properly: what stays and what goes.

## The rules

Two chores I'd already handed over, and one I tried from scratch over these ten days:

- **Scheduling:** planning my week in Google Calendar: which days I go to the gym, when I do schoolwork, which slots go to what, when I work on side projects.
- **Tasks:** instead of writing lists myself, I describe what needs doing and let AI list it back neatly.
- **Morning brief (new):** each morning, check whether I'm busy today, which slots are free, plus a few of the day's top stories.

One rule from the start: my current job stays out of reach. AI only handles my personal life, schoolwork and side projects.

Over the ten days I watched two things: how often I had to fix what the AI did, and whether I was still actually using it by day ten.

## Setup

Everything runs through Claude Code, connected to Google Calendar.

For scheduling, I give it the fixed blocks first: out the door at 7, work from 8, home around 7 in the evening. Then I tell it what needs to fit into the week: two gym sessions, about six hours of schoolwork, and time for four side projects. It finds the gaps and creates the events.

Tasks are simpler: I list whatever's floating around in my head, in no particular order, and the AI turns it into a tidy list.

The morning brief is the only part I had to build from scratch, so I saved it for day one.

## What the ten days looked like

### Mornings: the brief

Day one went into building the brief. From day two it became the thing I read before leaving home: what's on today, which slots are still free, and a few of the day's top stories.

The most useful part turned out to be the free slots. Since I only get home around 7 in the evening, knowing how much of the evening is free lets me decide in the morning: gym, schoolwork or side project tonight.

### Scheduling

This is the one I'm most used to. I don't have to work out which days the gym fits without clashing with everything else; I say two sessions this week, about six hours of schoolwork, which side project comes first, and let the AI find room on the calendar. With four side projects at once, splitting time between them is where I've found it helps most. When plans change, I ask it to move things instead of dragging events around myself.

### Tasks

Tasks are the lightest part. Whenever something comes to mind, I tell the AI instead of typing it into my phone, and it lists everything back neatly. Once the list is tidy, fitting it into the calendar gets easier too.

## Scorecard

| Task | Keep or drop | Why |
| ---- | ------------ | --- |
| Scheduling | Keep | No more juggling the gym, schoolwork and side projects around a day that's full from 7 to 7. |
| Tasks | Keep | Say it and it's done; no more scattered notes on paper and my phone. |
| Morning brief (new) | Keep | I know in the morning which evening slots are free. |

## What I learned

- **AI is best at the sorting.** I still decide what I want to do; the AI works out where it goes.
- **Give it the fixed blocks first.** Work hours and commute don't move. State them up front, and the AI can place everything else correctly.
- **Clear boundaries make it comfortable.** My current job stays out; the AI only touches my personal life, schoolwork and side projects.

## What's next

Beyond the daily chores, I'm exploring AI skills and applying them to my side projects, like the impeccable skill I used to rebuild this very site.
